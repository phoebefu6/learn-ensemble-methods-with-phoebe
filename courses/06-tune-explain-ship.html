Everything the course measured compresses into four moves, in order:

  • Start from defaults, shallow and slow. Depth 3, learning rate under 0.1, per the ESL law you verified in session 3. Defaults are better than folklore: the McElfresh finding was that LIGHT tuning of a gradient booster beats agonizing over model families.

Part 1 · covers explanation

Why did it flag ME? SHAP, the short honest version

8 min live

The voucher team will not act on a score they cannot interrogate, and the first escalation is always a named customer: why did it flag this one? For tree ensembles that question has an exact answer, computed in polynomial time - which is rarer than it sounds.

LiveWhat SHAP guarantees, and the tree-shaped luck4 min▶

SHAP - Lundberg and Lee, 2017 - assigns each feature a contribution to one specific prediction, borrowed from cooperative game theory, and proves it is the unique attribution satisfying a small set of consistency properties. Not "a" way to split the credit: the only way that keeps the books balanced - contributions sum exactly to the prediction's distance from the base rate.

The catch in general: computing Shapley values exactly is exponential. The tree-shaped luck: for tree ensembles, the 2020 TreeExplainer result gives "the first polynomial time algorithm to compute optimal explanations based on game theory" - exact values, fast, by walking the internal structure of the trees. Your booster gets exact explanations essentially free; a neural network gets approximations.

Two honesty rails for using it. Exact-in-polynomial-time is a TREES-ONLY privilege - KernelSHAP for arbitrary models approximates. And SHAP explains what the MODEL did, not what the world does: "73-day silence contributed +24" is a fact about the booster, a correlation the trees found, never proof that silence causes churn. Causal claims live on an entirely different ladder.

Self-studySettling your session-1 feature guesses3 min▶

Global SHAP - averaging attribution magnitudes across all customers - is the grown-up version of "which features matter". On the course data the ranking lands roughly: days since last order first by a wide margin, sessions and orders next, tickets and discount share behind, average order value nearly nothing.

Compare that to the guesses you wrote in session 1. The typical surprise is AOV: it feels like it should matter - expensive-basket customers feel loyal - and the trees, free to use it, barely touch it. An uninformative feature discovered by the model itself is session 1's first Grinsztajn reason, closing the loop.

Part 2 · covers the challenger

TabPFN: the challenger, taken seriously

6 min live

A course that crowned gradient boosting in its title owes you the strongest case against it. As of September 2026 that case is TabPFN, a tabular foundation model - and the honest verdict is genuinely split, along a line worth knowing precisely.

LiveHow to hold both columns at once4 min▶

The Nature result is real: on datasets up to 10,000 samples, TabPFN "outperforms all previous methods... by a wide margin", delivering in 2.8 seconds what took tuned baselines four hours. On data the size of this course's sample, the challenger is likely ahead - which is exactly why the final scorecard makes re-running it a standing row rather than a heresy.

The structural costs are also real. The envelope is hard - beyond it quality and memory degrade, which is why the vendor's own 2.5 release ships a distillation engine to convert predictions back into compact models. And in-context learning means every prediction re-processes the training data: a fitted booster scores a customer in microseconds forever, and that asymmetry is why production and competitions still belong to the trees.

Real world

The grown-up position in a 2026 architecture review: "GBDT is the shipped default; the foundation model is the quarterly challenger with a dated benchmark on our own data." That sentence contains no tribal loyalty, survives both futures, and costs one afternoon a quarter to maintain.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three for the final passport stamp.

1 · Tuned boosting (depth 3, rate 0.1, early-stopped) beat the identical algorithm run carelessly by 73 to 68. What produced the five points?

Same loop, same trees' worth of compute in the useful phase, same data. The difference is the ESL strategy: v small, M chosen by early stopping. The engine is deterministic - re-running it produces the same 73 and 68, which is exactly why the leaderboard can hard-code its expectations.

2 · What makes SHAP explanations for your booster special compared to explanations for an arbitrary model?

The 2020 TreeExplainer result: "the first polynomial time algorithm to compute optimal explanations based on game theory", exploiting tree structure. KernelSHAP for arbitrary models approximates. Exact, fast explanation is a genuine competitive advantage of shipping trees - and it explains the model, never the world: attributions are not causes.

3 · A colleague says "TabPFN beat XGBoost in Nature, the trees era is over." The course-grade response?

Both dismissal and surrender flunk. The peer-reviewed result is real and scoped; the in-the-wild record through 2024 is real and dated; the structural costs (size ceiling, per-prediction re-processing) are real and current. Holding contested evidence as contested, with dates, is the discipline - resolving it prematurely in either direction is the error.
